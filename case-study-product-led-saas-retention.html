The Problem

One Customer Journey, Five Disconnected Systems

A Fragmented Lifecycle: Signup and onboarding events live in the product analytics tool, trial state and plan changes live in billing, and churn reasons live in support notes — so no single query can answer "what actually happened to this customer, from signup to today."

Correlation Mistaken for Causation: Teams could see that certain early behaviors correlated with retention, but had no way to tell whether those behaviors caused retention, or simply selected for users who were already going to stick around.

Framework

The Connected Product Lifecycle

The model follows a single account and user from first touch through renewal, treating activation, trial conversion, paid retention, churn, and NRR as stages of one lifecycle rather than metrics owned by separate teams.

Signup→ Onboarding→ Activation→ Trial Conversion→ Paid Retention→ Churn / Expansion→ NRR

Approach

A Lifecycle Data Model & Experimentation Framework

1. Unified Lifecycle Model (SQL + dbt)

Modeled signup, onboarding events, trial state, subscription/billing status, and churn/support signals into one account-and-user-level lifecycle mart in dbt — with clearly defined activation, trial-conversion, and churn events so every downstream analysis uses the same definitions.

2. Activation Definition & Experimentation (Amplitude + Python)

  • Candidate Activation Milestones: Tested early actions — completing the core workflow, inviting a teammate, connecting an integration — for their relationship to 90-day retention.
  • A/B Tested Onboarding: Ran controlled experiments on onboarding flows (guided setup vs. self-serve) to validate which nudges causally move activation and trial conversion, rather than relying on cohort correlation alone.

3. Cohort & Survival Analysis (Python)

  • Kaplan-Meier Retention Curves: Modeled retention by cohort and activation tier to identify where and when accounts are most likely to churn.
  • NRR Waterfall: Decomposed net revenue retention into expansion, contraction, and churn by cohort to connect product behavior back to revenue.

Key Findings

Activation Tier Drives Conversion and Long-Term Retention

Segmenting trial users by activation behavior revealed a large, consistent gap between cohorts — reaching the core activation milestone during the trial was the strongest predictor of both paid conversion and 12-month retention.

Cohort Segment Trial→Paid Conv. 12-Month Retention NRR Contribution Primary Churn Driver
Signed Up, Not Activated 2.4% 19% −8% No core workflow completed in first session
Activated, Solo Use 9.1% 44% 3% No team or collaboration signal
Activated + Team Invite 27.6% 68% +19% Seat / usage-limit expansion trigger

Accounts that combined activation with a team invite converted at roughly 3.1× the rate of non-activated signups and retained at more than 3× the rate — confirming that the onboarding moment, not the marketing channel, was the highest-leverage intervention point.
