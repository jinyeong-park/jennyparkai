Five Metrics I Use to Measure Brand Visibility Inside AI Answers

A single ChatGPT prompt tells you almost nothing — the model is non-deterministic by design. Credible AI visibility measurement looks more like a sampling program than a rank checker.

The Measurement Problem

You can't rank-track a system that answers differently every time

Traditional SEO measurement assumes a stable, queryable object: a URL sits at position 4 for a keyword, and it mostly stays there until something changes. AI answer engines don't offer that stability. Ask the same model the same question twice and you can get two different answers — different phrasing, different sources cited, sometimes a different brand entirely.

That means a single prompt run tells you almost nothing about actual visibility. The methodology that holds up is closer to a polling program: run a standardized set of prompts across each engine, repeated 3–5 times per week to smooth out stochastic variation, and measure the pattern rather than any one response.

Structuring the prompt matrix

I organize prompts into four intent clusters, because a brand's visibility profile is rarely uniform across them:

  • Category discovery — "What are the best tools for [category]?" Tests whether the model considers the brand relevant at all.
  • Comparison — "Brand A vs. Brand B for [use case]." Tests competitive positioning head-to-head.
  • Use case / problem-solving — "How do I [job to be done]?" Tests whether the brand surfaces for the problem, not just the category label.
  • Decision / evaluation — "Is Brand A worth it for a [segment] team?" Tests whether the model actively endorses or just lists.
The Five Metrics

What I actually track per run

MetricDefinitionWhat it isolates
Mention RatePrompts with brand mention / total promptsBaseline relevance — is the brand in the consideration set at all
Recommendation RatePrompts with explicit endorsement / total promptsActive recommendation vs. passive inclusion in a list
AI Share of VoiceBrand mentions / all mentions in categoryCompetitive dominance relative to named rivals
Linked Citation RatePrompts with a clickable brand URL / total promptsWhether visibility can actually convert into traffic
PAWC (Position-Adjusted Word Count)Σ word count / log₂(position + 1)Depth and prominence of coverage, not just presence

Mention Rate and Recommendation Rate look similar on a chart but tell you different stories. A brand can appear in 60% of category-discovery answers while being actively recommended in only 15% of them — present in the room, but not the one being pointed to. That gap is usually the highest-leverage thing to close, because it's a content and proof-point problem, not a visibility problem.

Beyond the Prompt

Auditing the source mix behind every answer

None of these five metrics explain why a model says what it says. For that, I log the source mix cited alongside the brand in every response — review platforms (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius), earned media and industry press, community consensus (Reddit, Stack Overflow, domain forums), and owned documentation.

This is where the actual prioritization signal lives. If competitors are consistently sourced from G2 review volume and your brand's G2 profile is thin, that's a specific, fundable gap — not a vague "improve AI visibility" initiative. Source-mix auditing is what turns five numbers into a work plan.
