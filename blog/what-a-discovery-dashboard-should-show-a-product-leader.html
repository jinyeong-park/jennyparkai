What a Discovery Dashboard Should Actually Show a Product Leader

Most SEO dashboards are keyword tables wearing a leadership costume. A dashboard built for a product leader needs to answer one question first: is total organic reach growing revenue, or just growing?

The Wrong Default

Granular keyword tables are for the operator, not the leader

The most common mistake in discovery reporting is optimizing the executive view for completeness instead of decision-usefulness. A product leader doesn't need every tracked keyword's position delta — they need to know whether the combined discovery motion across web search, AI answer engines, and app stores is growing, and whether it's converting.

I build these dashboards in four layers, each answering a different question for a different reader.

Layer 1

Macro-discovery and revenue — four numbers, one screen

  • Unified Organic Reach — combined impressions across Google SERP, AI answer engines, and App Store search views. One trend line instead of three.
  • Blended Organic Conversion Volume — signups or purchases from organic web, the custom AI channel, and app installs, combined.
  • AI Referral Cohort LTV — 30- and 90-day LTV for AI-sourced visitors against traditional organic and paid, side by side.
  • Attribution Health Index — the percentage of traffic still sitting in the custom AI Search channel vs. leaking into unassigned or Direct, flagging referrer-stripping drift before it distorts everything downstream.
Layer 2

Surface-specific modules — where the operators actually live

Below the summary, each surface gets its own module with its own vocabulary, because collapsing them into one KPI set is how nuance gets lost.

ModuleCore KPIs
GEOCitation Share, AI SOV, Recommendation vs. Mention Rate, Linked Citation Frequency, source mix

ASOTTI, CPP conversion rate, Android Vitals health, keyword rank, D1/D30 retention curves
Layers 3 & 4

Competitive context, and the alerts that protect the pipeline

Layer 3 — competitive benchmarking. Head-to-head AI Share of Voice against the top three competitors, a prompt gap analysis surfacing exactly where they're recommended and you're absent, and app store keyword-rank deltas. This is what turns "we're doing fine" into "we're losing ground on comparison prompts specifically."

Layer 4 — operational risk alerts. The layer most dashboards skip entirely, and the one that prevents silent damage: an Android Vitals threshold warning before demotion hits, an AI Model Volatility alert on any Citation Share drop greater than 15% following a model release, an LLM crawler accessibility monitor watching for 4xx/5xx errors or accidental robots.txt blocks, and an /llms.txt parsing health check confirming the markdown mirrors are still valid.

The test I use for any dashboard row: would a specific person take a specific action differently because this number moved? If the answer is no, it belongs in the operator's tool, not the leader's screen.
