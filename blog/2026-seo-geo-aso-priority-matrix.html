The 2026 Priority Matrix: What to Fix First Across SEO, GEO, and ASO

Four initiatives take under a week each and fix the biggest blind spots most teams have. Two more take a quarter and build the moat. Everything else can wait — or should be avoided outright.

The Framework

Impact vs. effort, applied across three surfaces at once

Once a team accepts that SEO, GEO, and ASO are three distinct disciplines rather than one "search" line item, the next problem is sequencing — there's now three roadmaps competing for the same sprint capacity. I sort every candidate initiative into four tiers, and the tiering holds up consistently across the SaaS and consumer-app teams I've worked with.

Tier 1 — Do This Month

High impact, low effort: the 30-day list

  • GA4 Custom AI Channel Grouping. Under 2 hours of configuration restores visibility into a channel that's likely converting at 4–5× organic — the single best effort-to-impact ratio on this entire list.
  • Deploy /llms.txt and markdown mirrors. Removes token-parsing friction for AI crawlers, directly improving citation accuracy for near-zero ongoing cost.
  • ASO title and subtitle keyword alignment. The highest-weighted fields on both app stores; eliminating duplication alone recovers wasted character budget.
  • Answer-capsule restructuring on key pages. Adding tight definitions, comparison tables, and statistical summaries near the top of existing pages — no new pages required.
Tier 2 — The Moat

High impact, high effort: worth the cross-functional lift

  • Third-party entity and review footprint. Over 90% of AI citations trace back to non-brand-owned sources — G2, Capterra, Reddit, industry press. This is the single largest lever on Recommendation Rate and AI SOV, and it's the one initiative that can't be shortcut with a configuration change.
  • Android Vitals remediation. Engineering-owned, growth-relevant — protects the app from algorithmic demotion that no amount of metadata work can offset.
  • Intent-matched Custom Product Pages. 5–10 variants targeting distinct feature queries, typically worth a 15–25% lift in tap-to-install.

Tier 3 — BigQuery warehouse integration and In-App Event cadence — is real, medium-impact work, but it's additive polish on a foundation that only pays off once Tiers 1 and 2 are actually in place.

Tier 4 — Stop Doing These

Low impact or negative, regardless of effort

Mass AI-generated content without primary data. Generative engines actively penalize unsourced, generic text — this doesn't just fail to help, it can actively suppress citation likelihood.

iOS long-description keyword stuffing. Apple does not index this field for search rank. Any hours spent here return exactly zero ranking movement.

Blocking AI crawlers out of traffic-cannibalization fear. This doesn't protect the brand — it just hands the category's AI Share of Voice to whichever competitor left their robots.txt open.
