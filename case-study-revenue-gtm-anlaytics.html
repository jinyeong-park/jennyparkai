Revenue Intelligence · GTM Analytics

Revenue Intelligence & GTM Analytics

Built a revenue intelligence system that connects CRM, billing, pipeline, and customer health data to show how booked revenue converts into collected cash, where revenue is at risk, and which teams need to act.

The analysis surfaced a $2.1M gap between bookings and collected cash, identified 136 stale pipeline deals, and created a shared decision layer for Finance, RevOps, Marketing, and Customer Success.

Project Snapshot

The Challenge

Finance, RevOps, Marketing, and Customer Success were each measuring a different part of the revenue lifecycle.

CRM showed bookings. Billing showed invoices and payments. Sales tracked pipeline. Customer Success tracked retention and account health.

Each metric was useful on its own, but there was no shared model showing where expected revenue was being created, lost, delayed, or put at risk.

Project Snapshot

The Approach

I designed a shared revenue model connecting acquisition, sales pipeline, contracts, billing, payments, and customer health.

On top of that model, I created decision-focused analytical views for Finance, Marketing, RevOps, and Customer Success — each answering a different business question from the same underlying source of truth.

82% Cash collection rate against CRM bookings
$2.1M Revenue gap quantified between bookings and cash
136 Pipeline deals with no activity for 90+ days

The Problem

Every Team Had a Number. No One Had the Full Revenue Story.

01

Fragmented revenue definitions
CRM bookings, invoices, cash collection, weighted pipeline, and recurring revenue were measured independently.

02

Pipeline risk was hidden
Old and inactive deals remained in open pipeline, making the headline pipeline number look stronger than the underlying opportunity set.

03

Revenue problems lacked ownership
Discounts, billing gaps, payment risk, lead matching, and renewal exposure required different teams to act, but the reporting did not make that ownership clear.

The core issue was not simply missing dashboards. CRM, billing, pipeline, and customer data had never been connected into a shared analytical model.

The issue was not reporting. It was decision quality.

Decision Model

One Revenue Model, Multiple Business Decisions

I did not design separate views simply to create more dashboard pages. Each view exists because a different revenue question has a different decision owner.

Executive / Finance
How much booked revenue actually becomes collected cash?

Marketing / GTM
Where are potential customers being lost before revenue is created?

RevOps
Which open opportunities are realistically closeable, and which require intervention?

Customer Success
Which existing customers represent the greatest future recurring revenue risk?

Same customer. Same revenue lifecycle. Different decisions.

Data Model

Connecting GTM Activity to Revenue

I used dbt to standardize and connect data across the full B2B revenue lifecycle:

Marketing & Leads  →  Accounts  →  Opportunities  →  Contracts  →  Invoices  →  Payments  →  Customer Health

Staging Layer

Standardized field names, data types, null handling, and core identifiers across raw CRM, billing, product, and support data.

Intermediate Layer

Resolved lead-to-account relationships, calculated lifecycle timelines, scored pipeline risk, and combined product and support signals into customer health measures.

Analytics Marts

Created decision-ready datasets for revenue reconciliation, GTM funnel analysis, pipeline risk, and customer health.

Streamlit was used as the delivery layer so business users could explore these analytical models interactively. The core of the project is the shared revenue logic underneath the interface.

Why It Matters

The key insight was not simply that a $2.1M gap existed. The analysis separated the gap into causes with different operational owners.

That turns a financial variance into a set of specific decisions: review discount policy, resolve unbilled contracts, and prioritize collection risk.

Decision

Referral traffic showed the strongest conversion while Paid Search generated high volume with weaker downstream performance.

The implication was not simply “spend more on referrals.” It suggested reviewing qualification and lead quality before increasing paid acquisition spend.

Data Quality Finding

Fourteen percent of leads had no account ID, preventing those leads from being connected to downstream pipeline and revenue.

Before channel performance could be trusted, lead-to-account identity resolution needed to be improved.

Pipeline Intelligence

Raw Pipeline Is Not the Same as Closeable Revenue

CRM stage alone can overstate pipeline quality, so I created deterministic risk indicators that RevOps could understand, validate, and act on.

  • Past Due: expected close date has passed.
  • Stale: no recent opportunity activity.
  • Repeated Slip: close date has moved multiple times.

Key Finding

136 open deals had no recorded activity for more than 90 days. They were still visible in headline pipeline even though their current closeability was questionable.

Decision

RevOps could use the risk view to review stale deals, clean the active opportunity set, and distinguish raw pipeline from a more realistic weighted forecast.

Instead of asking only which accounts had low health scores, the model reframed the question:

This supports an intervention queue based on both customer risk and ARR exposure rather than account size alone.
