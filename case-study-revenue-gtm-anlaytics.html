Overview

Revenue data rarely lives in one system. Marketing owns leads and campaigns, Sales owns accounts and opportunities, Finance owns billing and revenue, Product owns usage, and Customer Success owns renewal, expansion, and churn.

Each system answers a different question. The harder question is how to connect the entire customer lifecycle into one trusted view of revenue.

“Connect GTM activity to revenue — from the first lead to pipeline, bookings, billing, recognized revenue, and retention.”

This project builds a Revenue Intelligence & GTM Analytics framework for a scaling B2B technology company. The model is intentionally designed around a common revenue lifecycle rather than a single source system or one narrow business model.

Business Problem

A scaling B2B organization can have multiple teams reporting on the same commercial performance while using different source systems and definitions.

MarketingWhich campaigns generate qualified demand?
SalesHow much pipeline exists and how likely is it to convert?
FinanceWhy do bookings, billings, and recognized revenue differ?
ProductDoes product adoption correlate with customer value?
Customer SuccessWhich accounts expand, renew, or churn?
LeadershipWhere should GTM resources be allocated next?
The analytical challenge: build a trusted foundation that preserves the differences between commercial metrics instead of forcing every source into one “revenue” number.

Business Objective

The objective is not to build another dashboard. It is to create a trusted analytical foundation that allows GTM, Sales, Finance, and leadership teams to answer:

  • Where is revenue coming from?
  • Where does the GTM funnel break?
  • Which pipeline is most likely to convert?
  • Why are bookings and revenue different?
  • Which customers and segments create the most long-term value?
  • What should the business do next?

The Core Revenue Lifecycle

The analytical model follows a common B2B lifecycle and explicitly separates concepts that are often incorrectly treated as the same metric.

Marketing / Demand Generation ↓ Lead ↓ Account ↓ Opportunity ↓ Contract / Order ↓ Billing ↙ ↘ Bookings Billings ↘ ↙ Recognized Revenue ↓ Expansion / Churn ↓ Customer Value / NRR
MetricBusiness meaningWhy it matters
PipelinePotential future businessMeasures future commercial opportunity.
BookingsContracted / won businessMeasures committed commercial value.
BillingsCustomer invoicingMeasures amounts billed to customers.
Recognized RevenueRevenue recognized for financial reportingProvides the financial performance view.
ExpansionAdditional value from existing customersShows growth within the installed base.
ChurnLost customer valueShows retention leakage and NRR pressure.

Revenue Model Coverage

The framework is designed to be business-model agnostic. The same analytical lifecycle can be adapted to different B2B revenue structures.

SubscriptionContract → subscription → billing → MRR / ARR → recognized revenue.
AdvertisingAdvertiser → campaign/order → media spend → delivered revenue.
TransactionalBuyer/account → transaction → order value → take-rate revenue.
The current synthetic dataset includes multiple revenue-model values so the analytical framework can be tested beyond a single commercial motion.

Synthetic Data Strategy

Synthetic data is used to reproduce the analytical conditions of a fragmented revenue environment without depending on confidential company data.

The generator intentionally creates imperfect records so the project can demonstrate data investigation, validation, reconciliation, and governance rather than only clean-table aggregation.

Identity IssuesDuplicate account records, missing account IDs, and orphan opportunities.
Commercial IssuesDuplicate contract IDs and billing amount/timing mismatches.
Lifecycle IssuesClose-date changes, failed/pending payments, and incomplete relationships.

Data Quality & Identity Resolution

Data quality is treated as part of analytics rather than a separate engineering concern. Before metrics reach a dashboard, the model should establish reliable keys, expected relationships, valid amounts, and defensible grain.

Identity resolution

  • Identify duplicate account records.
  • Resolve customer/account relationships across sources.
  • Flag records with missing or orphaned identifiers.
  • Prevent duplicate customer records from inflating metrics.

Metric integrity

  • Validate opportunity → contract relationships.
  • Compare contract values with billing activity.
  • Investigate invoice timing and amount mismatches.
  • Separate data exceptions from legitimate business variation.

Analytical Architecture

The transformation layer follows a dbt-style architecture that separates source cleanup, reusable business logic, and business-facing models.

Raw Sources ↓ Staging ↓ Intermediate Business Logic ↓ Facts + Dimensions ↓ Business Marts ↓ Dashboard / Analysis / AI
StagingStandardize names, data types, dates, identifiers, and source conventions.
IntermediateResolve identities and lifecycle relationships; prepare reusable logic.
MartsExpose pipeline, bookings, billing, revenue, funnel, and customer lifecycle metrics.

Revenue Reconciliation

Revenue reconciliation is designed as an investigation framework. The objective is not to force all numbers to match, but to explain why different commercial layers legitimately differ or where data-quality issues exist.

ComparisonInvestigation
Opportunity → ContractClosed-won opportunity value versus contract value.
Contract → BillingExpected commercial value versus invoiced amounts and timing.
Billing → RevenueInvoice activity versus the applicable recognition period.
Customer LifecycleExpansion and churn movements versus the customer revenue base.

GTM Funnel & Pipeline Intelligence

Pipeline risk is not determined by one CRM field. The analysis combines operational and historical signals to produce an explainable view of opportunity risk.

Lead Volume→ MQL / SQL→ Opportunity→ Pipeline→ Win Rate→ Revenue

Potential risk signals include:

  • Days in current stage
  • Total days open
  • Close-date changes
  • Time since last activity
  • Deal size
  • Historical stage conversion
  • Account segment
  • Product engagement
Example decision: rather than simply reporting a large enterprise pipeline, identify whether the opportunities have sufficient activity, realistic close dates, healthy stage progression, and historical conversion support.

Semantic Layer

A reliable analytics platform requires more than clean tables. It requires consistent business definitions.

Raw Data ↓ Business Logic ↓ Validated Metrics ↓ Semantic Layer ↙ ↓ ↘ Dashboard Analysis AI

The semantic layer should document metric name, business definition, calculation logic, filters, grain, source, ownership, valid use cases, and known limitations.

This creates a shared analytical language across Sales, Finance, GTM Strategy, Leadership, BI, and AI workflows.

AI-Ready Analytics

AI is treated as a consumer of governed business data, not as a replacement for the analytical foundation.

Business Question ↓ Semantic Context ↓ Validated Metrics ↓ Approved SQL ↓ Warehouse ↓ Results ↓ Business Insight ↓ Recommendation

The objective is to make the revenue data foundation AI-ready by providing consistent metric definitions, business context, validated calculations, controlled data access, and explainable analytical results.

Current Scope & Limitations

This portfolio project uses synthetic production-like data. The generated data is intended to demonstrate analytical architecture, data-quality investigation, revenue modeling, and decision workflows; it does not represent actual company performance.

At the current stage, this case study describes the analytical framework and intended decision outputs. Quantitative business findings should be added only after the raw data, transformations, reconciliation logic, and analyses have been executed and validated.

Key Takeaway

The goal is not to build a complicated data pipeline. The goal is to create a trusted analytical foundation that helps the business make better revenue decisions.

The project demonstrates how a business analyst can move from fragmented GTM data to business-ready analytics: understand the commercial lifecycle, define metrics, establish data quality, model reusable logic, investigate revenue and pipeline performance, and ultimately connect findings to decisions.
