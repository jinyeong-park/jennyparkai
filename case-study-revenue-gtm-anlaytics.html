1. Executive Summary

B2B revenue data rarely lives in one clean system. Marketing measures demand, Sales manages opportunities, Contracts and Billing capture commercial activity, Product records usage, Customer Success monitors retention, and Finance owns the recognized-revenue view.

This case study designs an end-to-end Revenue Intelligence foundation that connects those domains while explicitly separating Pipeline, Bookings, Billings, Recognized Revenue, Expansion, and Churn. The goal is not simply to build dashboards, but to establish a reliable analytical layer that answers business questions consistently.

Core business problem: Different teams can report different versions of “revenue performance” because the underlying records, identifiers, dates, and metric definitions are fragmented.

3. Revenue Lifecycle

The analytical model follows the commercial lifecycle rather than treating each source as an isolated dataset.

Marketing / Demand→ Lead→ Account→ Opportunity→ Contract / Order→ Billing→ Recognized Revenue→ Expansion / Churn

A key modeling principle is to avoid using one commercial amount as a proxy for every revenue concept. Pipeline represents potential future business; bookings represent committed business; billings represent invoiced amounts; recognized revenue follows the appropriate recognition logic; expansion and churn describe changes in the customer relationship.

4. Synthetic Source Data

The project begins with a Python generator that creates reproducible, intentionally imperfect B2B revenue data suitable for dbt/BigQuery analysis. The generated source layer contains seven CSV datasets.

SourcePurposeKey analytical role
Marketing LeadsLead acquisition and lifecycleChannel, campaign, lead-to-pipeline analysis
Salesforce AccountsCustomer/account masterIdentity, segmentation, lifecycle
Salesforce OpportunitiesSales pipelineStage, amount, forecast, conversion
ContractsCommercial agreementsBookings and contract reconciliation
BillingInvoices and paymentsBillings, collections, reconciliation

The generator deliberately includes duplicate account records, missing lead account identifiers, orphan opportunities, duplicate contract IDs, billing amount/timing mismatches, failed or pending payments, multiple revenue models, close-date changes, and incomplete lifecycle coverage. These are analytical inputs for data-quality work, not accidental defects.

5. Data Quality & Identity Resolution

Identity problems

  • Duplicate account records can inflate customer counts and revenue.
  • Missing identifiers prevent clean downstream joins.
  • Orphan opportunities create pipeline that cannot be tied to an account.
  • Duplicate contract IDs can double-count commercial value.

Metric integrity problems

  • Billing dates can differ from contract dates.
  • Billing amounts can differ from expected commercial values.
  • Payment status changes the interpretation of invoiced value.
  • Close-date changes affect pipeline timing and forecast analysis.

The analytical challenge is therefore not only “join the tables.” It is to establish a defensible customer identity, define grain, document business rules, and expose exceptions instead of silently hiding them.

6. Analytical Architecture

The intended transformation approach uses a layered dbt architecture, separating raw source data from cleaned staging models, reusable intermediate logic, and business-facing marts.

RawSource extracts generated as production-like CSVs.
StagingStandardize names, types, dates, identifiers, and source conventions.
IntermediateResolve identities, lifecycle relationships, and reconciliation logic.
FactsDefine measurable business events at explicit grain.
DimensionsAccount, customer, segment, region, channel, and time context.
MartsFunnel, pipeline, revenue, customer lifecycle, and executive reporting.

7. Reconciliation Framework

Revenue reconciliation is treated as an investigation framework rather than a single formula. Each commercial layer should be traceable to its source records and business definition.

LayerPrimary questionExample diagnostic
PipelineWhat business is expected?Open opportunity amount by stage and expected close period
BookingsWhat business became committed?Closed-won opportunities versus contract value
BillingsWhat was invoiced?Invoice amount and timing versus contract terms
Recognized RevenueWhat should be recognized?Revenue by recognition period and contract term
ExpansionWhere did customer value increase?Expansion amount by account and renewal cycle
ChurnWhere did customer value decline?Churned accounts and reasons

8. Business Analysis Workflow

1

Start with the business question

Define the decision, metric, population, time period, and grain before writing SQL.

2

Map the data requirement

Identify source systems, entities, keys, timestamps, and fields needed to answer the question.

3

Validate data quality

Test uniqueness, completeness, referential integrity, valid dates, and expected business relationships.

4

Model reusable metrics

Build consistent transformations so different reports do not recreate competing metric definitions.

5

Analyze and investigate

Use funnel, pipeline, revenue, usage, and customer-health views to identify drivers and anomalies.

6

Communicate the decision

Translate findings into concise business implications, risks, and recommended actions.

10. Key Takeaways

The value of Revenue Intelligence is not simply producing more dashboards. It is creating a trusted analytical foundation that connects fragmented GTM and revenue processes, makes metric definitions explicit, surfaces data-quality issues, and gives business teams a common language for revenue performance.

This project demonstrates a business-first analytics workflow: start from the revenue lifecycle, build the data foundation, validate its integrity, model reusable metrics, investigate performance, and communicate the implications.
