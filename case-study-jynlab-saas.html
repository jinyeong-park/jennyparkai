B2B SaaS Customer Lifecycle & Cohort Retention Analysis

A freemium B2B SaaS platform experienced declining Net Revenue Retention and unpredictable free-to-paid conversions. This project built an enterprise analytics framework in dbt and Snowflake, transforming granular product usage logs into a unified SaaS metrics datamart — then applied survival analysis to identify the exact onboarding moments that determine long-term retention.

The Problem

Declining NRR with No Visibility into Why

Declining NRR & Churn: The VP of Growth lacked visibility into which user onboarding pathways led to long-term enterprise conversion versus early churn — making it impossible to design effective intervention strategies.

Siloed Data: Product activity logs were disconnected from subscription revenue data, preventing cross-functional alignment between Product, Marketing, and Finance on shared metrics like MRR, ARR, and NRR.

Approach

Data Architecture & Analytical Approach

1. Data Transformation & Analytics Engineering (dbt + Snowflake)

Built a modular data pipeline transforming raw product events and billing data into production-ready analytical models:

  • Feature Adoption & Activation: Quantified Product-Qualified Lead (PQL) milestones such as team invites and core workspace actions to define activation thresholds.
  • SaaS Revenue Datamart: Modeled Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR), Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR), Churn Rate, and Net Revenue Retention (NRR) as standardized, cross-functional metrics in Snowflake.

2. Survival Analysis & Aha Moment Identification (Python)

  • Kaplan-Meier Survival Estimates: Utilized Python (lifelines) to model user retention curves and predict precise churn windows across customer cohorts segmented by activation behavior.
  • Activation Lever Mapping: Identified that inviting 3 or more teammates within the first 7 days served as the core "Aha moment," increasing 90-day retention by over 2.5×.

Key Findings

Cohort Performance by Activation Tier

Segmenting users by activation behavior revealed a dramatic performance gap — the difference between a 1.2% and 18.8% free-to-paid conversion rate came down to a single onboarding action.

Cohort Segment Free-to-Paid Conv. 90-Day Retention ARR Expansion Rate Primary Churn Driver
Non-Activated Users 1.2% 14.0% 0.0% Low core feature adoption within 3 days
Single-User Accounts 4.5% 38.0% 5.2% Lack of team collaboration triggers
PQL Activated (3+ Invites) 18.8% 82.5% 34.1% Seats limit reached (Expansion trigger)

PQL-activated users converted at 15× the rate of non-activated users and retained at nearly 6× the rate — confirming that the onboarding "Aha moment" was the highest-leverage intervention point in the entire funnel.
