Project snapshot

Turning fragmented GTM inputs into structured decisions.

GTM teams rarely lack information. They lack a consistent way to separate what they know, what they assume, what matters, and what should be tested next.

The challenge

Strategy is often built across disconnected evidence.

Market research, customer interviews, competitor analysis, analytics, pricing assumptions, launch plans, and stakeholder opinions often live in different systems with no clear decision trail.

The approach

Make the reasoning behind GTM decisions explicit.

The system separates evidence, assumptions, interpretation, recommendations, and human decisions so each strategic choice can be challenged, validated, and updated.

Problem

Most GTM work has a traceability problem.

A polished strategy can still be weak if the evidence underneath it is unclear. Generative AI increases that risk because unsupported recommendations can sound just as confident as well-supported ones.

01 Evidence gets fragmented

Research, analytics, customer feedback, and stakeholder inputs often live in separate tools and documents.

02 Assumptions become facts

Founder beliefs and early hypotheses can quietly become strategic inputs without sufficient validation.

03 Recommendations lose context

Teams may know what was recommended without knowing why, based on which evidence, or under which assumptions.

04 Learning loops break

Launch results and customer behavior do not always flow back into the next positioning, channel, or growth decision.

The goal is not to automate strategy. The goal is to make GTM decisions more evidence-backed, traceable, and testable.

Decision framework

Five questions organize the system.

Rather than generating one large GTM strategy, the product breaks the work into a sequence of connected decisions.

01
Where are we?
Diagnose whether the product is still validating product-market fit or is ready for a scaling-oriented GTM approach.
02
Where should we compete?
Evaluate markets, customer segments, evidence quality, pain severity, reachability, and opportunity.
03
How should we win?
Develop positioning, value proposition, pricing, messaging, and channel hypotheses tied to the priority customer.
04
How should we launch?
Translate strategy into launch priorities, ownership, milestones, KPIs, dependencies, and approval decisions.
05
What should we improve next?
Feed funnel, cohort, revenue, attribution, and experiment evidence back into the next strategic decision.
Design principles

AI supports the system. It does not own the decision.

The product architecture intentionally separates evidence, deterministic logic, AI interpretation, and human judgment.

01 / EVIDENCE

Evidence before recommendation

Important recommendations should be tied to evidence. Unsupported inputs remain assumptions instead of quietly becoming facts.

02 / LOGIC

Deterministic rules before AI judgment

Scores, thresholds, calculations, workflow states, and business rules belong to deterministic application logic.

03 / CONTROL

Human approval for consequential decisions

ICP selection, pricing, launch readiness, public claims, and major budget decisions require explicit human review.

04 / TRACEABILITY

Every important decision should be explainable

A recommendation should be traceable back to its sources, assumptions, interpretation, and final human decision.

Operating modes

The system adapts to the amount of evidence available.

A team does not need a finished product or historical revenue data to begin. What changes is the type of conclusion the system is allowed to make.

Mode 01

Idea Validation

For teams with an early product or business hypothesis but little or no internal performance data.

  • Structure assumptions
  • Review available market evidence
  • Identify critical unknowns
  • Create proto-segments
  • Build a validation backlog
  • Design research and experiments
Mode 02

Evidence-Backed GTM

For teams with customer, product, revenue, or marketing evidence that can support stronger strategic decisions.

  • Compare target segments
  • Score ICP candidates
  • Develop positioning hypotheses
  • Evaluate channel strategy
  • Build measurable launch plans
  • Connect strategy to observed results
Evidence model

Not every statement deserves the same level of certainty.

The system explicitly labels material statements so users can see which parts of a strategy are known, inferred, assumed, or still unknown.

Verified fact
Directly supported by a credible source or supplied dataset.
Inference
A reasonable interpretation derived from the available evidence.
Assumption
An unverified input temporarily used for planning.
Recommendation
A proposed action based on stated evidence and assumptions.
Unknown
Important information that is not currently available.

Confidence is separate from strategic importance. A high-impact claim with weak evidence becomes a validation priority—not a confident recommendation.

Workflow

From product context to an approved decision.

01
Intake
Collect product, market, customer, pricing, business model, objectives, resources, and available evidence.
02
Stage diagnosis
Determine whether the analytical focus should be validation or scaling.
03
Evidence mapping
Separate verified evidence, assumptions, inferences, and unknowns.
04
Segment evaluation
Compare ICP candidates using transparent weighted criteria such as pain, fit, urgency, reachability, and market potential.
05
Strategy hypotheses
Generate positioning, messaging, pricing, and channel hypotheses appropriate to the available evidence.
06
Human approval
Review the evidence behind material decisions before strategy or launch outputs are finalized.
07
Learning loop
Feed observed customer and performance evidence back into the next prioritization and experiment cycle.
System boundaries

Rules calculate. AI interprets. Humans decide.

Keeping these responsibilities separate makes the system easier to evaluate, audit, and trust.

Evaluation

Measure decision quality—not just output quality.

Evaluation is designed to test whether the system is grounded, traceable, useful, and appropriately uncertain.

01 Evidence coverage

What percentage of material claims can be traced to supporting evidence?

02 Unsupported-claim rate

How often does the system make claims that exceed the available evidence?

03 Human revision rate

How much of the generated recommendation requires substantive correction?

04 Recommendation acceptance

Which recommendations survive human review and why?

05 Traceability

Can users understand which evidence and assumptions produced the recommendation?

06 Actionability

Does the output lead to a clear, measurable next decision or validation step?
