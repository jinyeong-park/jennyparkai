Lead Funnel Analytics

Built a full-stack analytics system for an insurance performance-marketing business — connecting Google, Meta, and Affiliate spend to verified partner revenue across 7,795 leads, 6 months, and 4 insurance verticals.

The system uncovered 15.2% platform overclaim (platforms claiming 6,795 leads vs. 5,764 verified in warehouse), identified 2 of 5 campaigns operating below break-even, and showed every partner below the 80% acceptance target — turning a CPL-optimized operation into a revenue-first one.

Project Snapshot

The Business

An insurance lead generation company buys demand from Google, Meta, and affiliates — then routes each submitted lead to insurance carriers (Progressive, State Farm, Allstate, etc.) one partner at a time in priority order until one accepts.

Only accepted leads generate revenue. Every rejected or capacity-exceeded lead is pure acquisition cost. Partner payouts range from $58 to $128 per accepted lead.

The critical question: which acquisition channels generate the most accepted, monetized leads — not just the most submissions?

Project Snapshot

The Problem

Marketing was optimizing toward platform-reported CPL. But platforms systematically overclaim: a campaign reporting 144 conversions at $8,795 revenue could yield only 132 warehouse leads — 28 of which fail validation — leaving just 64 accepted leads and $3,200 in verified revenue.

The business had no single system connecting acquisition spend to downstream partner acceptance and verified cash. Each team — marketing, operations, partner management — measured only their own stage.

$356K Total verified revenue across 6 months · 1.61x blended portfolio ROAS

The Problem

Cheap Leads Can Cost More Than Expensive Ones

01

CPL ≠ Revenue per lead
Affiliate traffic generated the lowest CPL ($17.50) and the highest ROAS (2.69x) — but also the worst valid-lead rate (59.1%). Google leads cost $44–$46 but converted at 80–82% valid rate. Optimizing toward CPL alone would systematically shift budget toward lower-quality supply.

02

Platform conversions are overstated
Meta campaigns overstated conversions by 15–40%. Google overclaimed by 5–20%. The blended overclaim rate across paid channels was 15.2%. Budget decisions made on platform metrics were working from systematically inflated signals.

03

No partner visibility
All 10 insurance partners were below the 80% acceptance target. Four were below the 70% operational minimum. Without visibility into which partners were rejecting leads — and why — routing priority couldn't be adjusted to protect revenue.

The team didn't need more dashboard tiles. They needed one connected system from acquisition spend to verified partner revenue.

The decision wasn't CPL. It was revenue per valid, accepted lead.

SQL Design: Preventing Fan-out

The mart pre-aggregates quote events and lead records separately into the same channel × vertical × month grain before joining — because a direct join would create one session row per quote event, inflating session counts by the number of events per user.

Without pre-aggregation, joining leads to ad spend records would inflate total spend by 3x.

The Affiliate Paradox

C005 (Affiliate) shows the highest ROAS at 2.69x — but the lowest valid-lead rate at 59.1%. This creates a deceptive signal: the channel looks profitable on blended metrics, but nearly 4 in 10 leads generated cost money to acquire and route without generating any revenue. Scale this channel without addressing quality and the economics deteriorate.

Attribution: First-Touch vs. Submitted Channel

The attribution mart (mart_attribution) applied QUALIFY ROW_NUMBER() = 1 ORDER BY event_timestamp ASC to isolate each lead's earliest quote event as the first-touch channel. Approximately 15% of leads showed a different first-touch channel than the submitted channel — cross-channel journeys that standard last-touch attribution would misattribute entirely to one channel.

Partner Performance

All 10 Partners Below the 80% Target. Four Below the 70% Minimum.

The partner mart (mart_partner_performance) tracked every routing attempt — delivered, accepted, rejected, capacity-exceeded — across 10 insurance partners. A key design decision: a 7-day rolling acceptance rate using RANGE BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW on UNIX_DATE(route_date) to distinguish genuine trend from one-day noise. RANGE (not ROWS) handles partner gaps — partners with only ~70 active days out of 180.

What the Rolling Rate Reveals

A partner's 6-month average acceptance rate can look stable while a 7-day trend shows a sharp recent decline. The rolling rate uses volume-weighted aggregation (SUM(accepted) / SUM(delivered) over the 7-day window) rather than an average of daily rates — which would overweight low-volume days. This distinguishes a genuine trend from a single high-rejection day.

Why Overclaim Exists

Platform conversions fire on ad clicks or page events — before lead validation, duplicate detection, or partner submission. The gap accumulates from: attribution window differences, pixel double-firing, cross-device mismatches, test traffic, and leads rejected as duplicates in the warehouse.

At 15.2% blended overclaim, any ROAS calculation using platform lead counts as the denominator overstates performance by that same margin. Budget decisions and campaign optimization need to work from warehouse-verified numbers.

Technical Architecture

Five SQL Marts. Five LookML Views. One Governed Semantic Layer.

The system is structured in three explicit layers so business logic lives in one place and teams can't accidentally create conflicting metric definitions in individual dashboard tiles.

BigQuery + SQL
5 Mart Tables, 1 Shared Grain per Mart
Each mart has a documented grain. Pre-aggregation CTEs prevent spend fan-out on joins. FULL OUTER JOIN handles organic/direct channels in reconciliation. QUALIFY ROW_NUMBER() isolates first-touch events per lead. SAFE_DIVIDE throughout.
LookML Semantic Layer
5 Views · 5 Explores · Reusable Metrics
Ratio measures defined as type: number with SAFE_DIVIDE(SUM_num, SUM_denom) — never average of a pre-computed ratio. Every view has a composite primary key to prevent fan-out on Explore joins. value_format_name set in LookML for format consistency.
Rolling Window Design
RANGE vs. ROWS — Calendar Days, Not Row Count
Partner rolling acceptance uses RANGE BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW on UNIX_DATE(route_date). RANGE calculates over calendar-day distance — partners with gaps in daily activity get correct 7-day windows. ROWS would silently skip non-consecutive dates.
Streamlit Dashboard
4 Pages · Plotly Charts · Alert Logic
Executive Summary → Lead Funnel → Campaign Performance → Partner & Reconciliation. Each page includes conditional alert callouts (partner below 70%, campaign below break-even, overclaim above 10% audit threshold). Sidebar filters by channel, vertical, and month range.
