The Problem

Signup Growth Was Masking a Lifecycle Leakage Problem

Activation Gap: 35% of accounts never created a workspace after signing up. Among those that did, fewer than half completed a key product action within 7 days. The team had no consistent definition of "activated" — and no way to connect early behavior to downstream paid conversion or churn.

Experiment Without an Analytic Layer: A new guided onboarding flow was running in production, but there was no readout connecting it to downstream outcomes. The team knew activation had improved — but not whether that improvement carried into paid conversion, retention, or segment-level impact.

Invisible Churn Risk: Customer success had no systematic way to prioritize at-risk accounts. Outreach was based on account size alone, not behavioral signals. 620 high-risk accounts had already churned or never converted, and another 1,112 medium-risk accounts with $96K in active MRR were showing declining engagement without triggering any action.

The core problem was not a reporting gap — it was structural. Product behavior data, subscription records, and experiment assignments had never been joined into a single lifecycle model. Each team saw their own slice. Nobody could see where accounts were dropping off, why, or whose accounts were next.

Approach

Behavioral Events → Lifecycle Metrics → Decision-Ready Dashboard

1. Shared Account-Level Metric Layer

Five raw tables — organizations, users, event logs, subscriptions, and experiment assignments — were joined into a single account-level record in build_account_metrics(). Every downstream page queries this shared layer, guaranteeing that activation rates, retention flags, revenue metrics, and churn scores all count the same accounts the same way.

2. Precise Metric Definitions

Activation required two things: workspace creation and at least one key product action within 7 days of signup. Workspace creation alone was treated as an onboarding milestone — not activation — to prevent the funnel from crediting accounts that were partially configured but had not reached product value. The same precision was applied throughout:

  • Retention — measured at 30/60/90 days for accounts whose paid-conversion date had passed the horizon at the shared observation date. Ineligible accounts were excluded from every denominator.
  • NRR — computed as retained cohort MRR / beginning cohort MRR per 60-day eligible cohort. Disclosed as a retained-MRR proxy because the dataset has no expansion events.
  • Churn Risk — rules-based scoring across four transparent signals: subscription status, activation, eligible retention, and recency. Documented explicitly as a prioritization tool, not a predictive model.

3. Test Coverage for Every Metric

23 unit and integration tests assert that each metric computes correctly against known-outcome fixtures — including edge cases where ineligible accounts would incorrectly inflate retention rates or churn scores. The tests verified both the metric definitions and the data generation logic after two data quality fixes were identified and corrected during development.

Segment-Level Heterogeneity

Activation lift was not uniform across segments. SMB and Mid-Market showed the largest absolute lifts from guided onboarding. Enterprise accounts showed a smaller, less certain response — likely because enterprise onboarding depends more on implementation support and dedicated CSM involvement than on in-product prompts.

Segment Control activation Variant activation Lift Rollout recommendation
SMB ~33% ~51% ~+18 pp Roll out immediately
Mid-Market ~34% ~53% ~+19 pp Roll out immediately
Enterprise ~36% ~43% ~+7 pp Separate path — monitor before rollout

The flat paid conversion result (−0.1 pp) is the most important downstream check: activation lift that does not translate into paid conversion or retention may reflect surface-level engagement rather than genuine product value. The data recommends treating paid conversion as a co-primary metric for the enterprise segment before committing to a full rollout.

Churn Risk & CS Prioritization

$96K in MRR Exposed in Medium-Risk Accounts — Before Any Churn Has Occurred

Rather than a black-box propensity model, churn risk uses four transparent signals that any customer success or product team can immediately verify and act on.

  • Subscription status: Churned (+55) or never converted (+30)
  • No 7-day activation: +20 points
  • Failed 60-day retention for eligible accounts: +15 points
  • Product recency gap: 31–90 days since last event (+10), 90+ days (+20)

Findings & Recommendations

Four Decisions the Dashboard Enables

Product — Roll Out Guided Onboarding to SMB and Mid-Market

+18.6 pp activation lift, p < 0.001 → SMB and Mid-Market rollout → Activation improvement at scale: Deploy the guided onboarding flow to SMB and Mid-Market accounts. Hold Enterprise in a controlled path — the lift is weaker and less certain, and enterprise onboarding benefits more from implementation support than in-product prompts.

Product — Move Teammate Invite Earlier in the Flow

Teammate invite → strongest retention predictor → Add to early onboarding: Accounts that invite teammates show meaningfully higher 60-day retention than those that do not. Moving the invite prompt to before workspace setup completes turns a collaboration event into an activation driver — not just a downstream engagement signal.

Analytics — Treat Paid Conversion as a Co-Primary Metric

Activation lift + flat conversion → metric guardrail → Avoid over-indexing on activation: The experiment improved activation by 18.6 pp but left paid conversion unchanged (−0.1 pp). Before extending the rollout or using activation as the success criterion for future onboarding experiments, add trial-to-paid conversion as a co-primary metric to confirm the lift carries through to monetisation.

Customer Success — Intervene in 1,112 Medium-Risk Accounts

$96,627 MRR + declining engagement → Scaled re-engagement → NRR protection: Run a scaled sequence for medium-risk accounts: use-case reminder email, training offer, and a product-adoption review call for the highest-MRR accounts within the segment. Set a 60-day survival rate target to measure the programme's impact.
