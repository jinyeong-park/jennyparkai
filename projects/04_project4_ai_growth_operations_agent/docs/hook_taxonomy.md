# Tablr Hook Taxonomy

**Document status:** Synthetic portfolio simulation. All examples, personas, and performance notes are hypothetical.

---

## Purpose

This taxonomy defines the five primary hook types used in Tablr's performance creative strategy. Each hook type maps to a psychological mechanism, funnel stage fit, persona alignment, and expected quality signal. It is the reference document for brief writers, creative producers, and anyone reviewing or generating Tablr ad creative.

A hook is the first 3–5 seconds of a video ad or the first headline of a static ad. It determines whether a restaurant owner stops scrolling. But the hook also sets expectations — it determines *who* stops scrolling and *why*, which shapes downstream activation quality.

---

## Hook Types

---

### 1. CONTRAST

**What makes this hook work psychologically**

CONTRAST exploits the gap between "what they have" and "what the competition has." For restaurant owners, that gap is acutely felt: they see large chain brands running coordinated promotions, loyalty apps, geo-targeted offers, and retargeting campaigns — and they're doing none of it. The CONTRAST hook names that gap without shaming the owner. It says: the playing field isn't fair, but it doesn't have to stay that way. The emotional trigger is competitive frustration turning into motivation.

**When to use**

- Funnel stage: awareness and consideration
- Best when the audience is problem-aware but hasn't evaluated solutions yet
- Strong at the top of funnel for cold audiences who haven't heard of Tablr
- Works when the gap between chains and independents is visible and real to the viewer

**When to avoid**

- Do not use with community_focused persona — they define themselves against genericness, not chains. A "chains vs. you" frame can feel like it's asking them to become more chain-like.
- Avoid if the creative execution feels accusatory ("You're losing") rather than empowering ("You can compete now")
- Do not name real restaurant chains in the creative — use "national brands" or "the big chains"
- Weak in retargeting where the audience already knows Tablr — they've moved past awareness

**3 example hooks (Tablr restaurant context)**

1. "The chain down the street has a loyalty app, a marketing team, and an automated email list. You have Instagram. That's about to change."
2. "National brands spend $2 million a year on customer acquisition. Independent restaurant owners spend Tuesday nights posting on Instagram. Tablr gives you the same tools — without the marketing department."
3. "They have a loyalty program, a mobile app, and a re-engagement campaign running right now. You have a great restaurant. Let's make that enough."

**Example opening 3 seconds (video)**

Owner is behind the counter looking at their phone. Cut to a split screen: a chain restaurant's polished loyalty app notification on one side, a blank restaurant owner's phone screen on the other. Text overlay: "They have the playbook. Now you do too."

**Best personas**

- scrappy_independent (primary — high resonance with the competitive frustration)
- delivery_heavy (secondary — "the apps are the chain" variant)
- growth_minded (secondary — ROI framing of the gap)

**Best channels**

- META (primary — broad reach, good for cold audiences)
- TIKTOK (secondary — native stop-scroll format, owner-led delivery)
- GOOGLE_SEARCH (secondary — captures "restaurant marketing software" intent queries)

**Expected CTR vs baseline**

- Meta: HIGH — CONTRAST is the top-performing hook type for scrappy_independent in synthetic data. Stops the scroll because the gap is immediately recognizable.
- TikTok: HIGH — native hook format, especially when delivered by an owner on camera
- Google Search: MEDIUM — intent is higher so the hook matters less, but the contrast framing in headlines sharpens the value prop

**Activation quality signal**

HIGH. CONTRAST hooks attract owners who are frustrated with their current situation and actively looking for a fix — not passive observers. They tend to arrive at the landing page with a clearer problem-solution frame, which correlates with higher onboarding completion. Watch for owners who click but don't complete onboarding: this may indicate the contrast was compelling but the product demo didn't match the promise.

### 2. OUTCOME

**What makes this hook work psychologically**

OUTCOME shows the end state the owner wants before explaining how to get there. The psychology is desire-first: paint the picture of full tables, loyal regulars, a direct ordering system that doesn't charge 30% commissions. Outcome hooks work because restaurant owners are outcome-oriented operators. They don't buy marketing software — they buy more customers, more revenue, and less chaos. The hook creates aspiration, then the body copy explains how Tablr delivers it.

**When to use**

- Funnel stage: awareness and consideration
- Best for audiences who are already goal-oriented (growth_minded, new_owner)
- Works well when the outcome is specific and credible, not vague ("grow your business")
- Strong for new owners who have a very clear, urgent outcome in mind

**When to avoid**

- Avoid vague outcomes that could apply to any business ("grow your revenue")
- Do not promise specific revenue percentages or guaranteed customer counts — these violate brand compliance rules
- Weak for community_focused persona who are outcome-skeptical about generic marketing promises
- Avoid in channels where trust is not yet established — outcome hooks need enough context to be credible

**3 example hooks (Tablr restaurant context)**

1. "Sixty days after launching Tablr, Maria's Mexican Kitchen had 340 loyalty members and a 22% repeat visit rate. Here's what she did in the first week."
2. "What does 100 loyal regulars actually look like? It looks like a full dining room on a Tuesday. Here's how new restaurant owners are building that base in their first year."
3. "Direct orders. Your customer list. Your margins. No delivery app commission. This is what owning your customer relationship looks like."

**Example opening 3 seconds (video)**

Time-lapse of a restaurant filling up from empty to packed. Owner looks at their phone and smiles. Cut to Tablr dashboard showing "127 new loyalty members this month." Text: "This is what growth looks like when it runs itself."

**Best personas**

- new_owner (primary — "first 100 loyal customers" outcome framing)
- growth_minded (primary — ROI and efficiency outcome)
- delivery_heavy (secondary — margin recovery outcome)

**Best channels**

- META (primary — broad reach, room to show outcome visually)
- TIKTOK (secondary — before/after format works well for outcome hooks)
- LINKEDIN (secondary — business outcome framing fits LinkedIn's professional context)

**Expected CTR vs baseline**

- Meta: MEDIUM-HIGH — strong for new_owner and growth_minded but not the top hook for scrappy_independent
- TikTok: HIGH — before/after and outcome reveal format is native to TikTok
- LinkedIn: MEDIUM — outcome framing resonates in professional context, but copy density needs to be higher

**Activation quality signal**

HIGH. Owners who click on a specific, credible outcome hook arrive with clear expectations. They activate at higher rates because they know what they want to accomplish. Watch for a mismatch between the outcome promised in the hook and the onboarding flow — if the hook says "100 loyal customers" but onboarding starts with analytics setup, expect a drop-off.

### 3. SOCIAL_PROOF

**What makes this hook work psychologically**

Social proof reduces perceived risk by showing that others — specifically, owners who look and sound like the viewer — have already made the decision and benefited from it. For restaurant owners, who are often solo operators making decisions without a support team, peer validation is particularly powerful. The key is specificity: "restaurant owners like you" is weak. "Miguel, who runs a 2-location taco spot in Austin" is strong. The more the viewer can see themselves in the proof, the faster the trust transfer.

**When to use**

- Funnel stage: awareness and consideration (cold), and consideration (warm/retargeting)
- Highest impact with scrappy_independent and new_owner — both rely heavily on peer evidence before committing
- Strong for community_focused persona when the proof comes from a restaurant with a similar local identity
- Use when the creative can feature a real-looking owner (UGC style, not polished brand)

**When to avoid**

- Avoid with growth_minded persona at top of funnel — they want metrics and ROI, not peer stories
- Avoid when the social proof feels staged or generic (stock-photo owner, vague claim)
- Weak on LinkedIn where professional credibility requires data-backed claims
- Do not use fabricated quotes or testimonials — all proof in this simulation is hypothetical

**3 example hooks (Tablr restaurant context)**

1. "I was ready to give up on marketing. Then another owner in my city told me about Tablr. Three weeks later I had 80 new loyalty members and my first sold-out Sunday brunch."
2. "Every owner I know who tried Tablr said the same thing: 'I wish I'd done this a year ago.' Here's what they found in the first 30 days."
3. "She runs a 14-table Italian spot by herself. No marketing team, no agency. She set up Tablr in an afternoon and launched her first campaign before dinner service."

**Example opening 3 seconds (video)**

Close-up on a restaurant owner's face — genuine, not polished. They're in their restaurant before service. They look at camera and say: "I'm not a marketing person. I'm a cook. But this actually worked."

**Best personas**

- scrappy_independent (primary)
- new_owner (primary)
- community_focused (strong — when proof comes from an authentic, neighborhood-style restaurant)

**Best channels**

- META (primary — UGC-style social proof performs well at scale on Meta)
- TIKTOK (primary — owner-led UGC is native to the platform format)

**Expected CTR vs baseline**

- Meta: MEDIUM-HIGH — UGC social proof regularly outperforms polished brand creative on Meta for this audience
- TikTok: HIGH — the format is native; an owner talking to camera is indistinguishable from organic content

**Activation quality signal**

HIGH for scrappy_independent and new_owner. Owners who are convinced by peer proof tend to arrive with higher motivation to activate — they've already seen someone "like them" succeed. However, watch for false-fit: if the proof story doesn't closely match the viewer's actual situation, they may sign up out of hope rather than genuine fit, leading to weaker onboarding completion.

### 4. DEMONSTRATION

**What makes this hook work psychologically**

DEMONSTRATION removes the "but does it actually work?" objection by showing the product in action before asking for any commitment. For skeptical restaurant owners who have been burned by tools that were hard to use or didn't deliver, seeing the product work is more convincing than any claim. The demonstration hook specifically shows simplicity, speed, and relevance — "look how easy this is to set up, and look what it did immediately." It converts skeptics who won't believe a headline.

**When to use**

- Funnel stage: consideration and acquisition
- Best for audiences who are solution_aware or product_aware — they know tools like this exist but haven't committed
- Growth_minded persona: they want to see capability, integrations, and output before committing
- Community_focused persona: they need to see that the tool won't make their brand feel generic
- YouTube is the primary channel — longer dwell time supports a full product walkthrough

**When to avoid**

- Avoid at top of funnel for cold, unaware audiences — they need to feel the problem first before they care about the solution
- Do not use a demonstration that shows too many features at once — simplicity is the point
- Weak on TikTok for polished product demos — the format feels mismatched; keep demos raw and quick
- Avoid when the product UI shown in the creative does not match the current product (can create expectation mismatch)

**3 example hooks (Tablr restaurant context)**

1. "Watch: an owner with zero marketing experience sets up a new customer acquisition campaign in Tablr in under 8 minutes. Start to finish."
2. "Here's exactly what happens when you connect your restaurant to Tablr for the first time. Minute one: your profile. Minute four: your first campaign live."
3. "We handed a restaurant owner Tablr with zero instructions. This is what they figured out in 12 minutes — and the campaign they launched by end of service."

**Example opening 3 seconds (video)**

Screen recording of Tablr dashboard opening — cursor moves to "Create Campaign." No narration yet. Text overlay: "0:00 — Zero marketing experience. Let's go." Then the owner's voice begins.

**Best personas**

- growth_minded (primary — wants to see capability and workflow)
- community_focused (secondary — needs to see the tool respects their brand identity)
- scrappy_independent (secondary — simplicity demonstration is compelling proof)

**Best channels**

- YOUTUBE (primary — format supports longer demonstration)
- LINKEDIN (secondary — product walkthrough fits professional evaluation mode)
- META (secondary — short-form demo clips, under 30 seconds)

**Expected CTR vs baseline**

- YouTube: HIGH — demonstration format is native to YouTube's evaluation context; viewers in consideration mode respond well
- LinkedIn: MEDIUM — professional audience is receptive to capability demonstrations
- Meta: MEDIUM — effective but needs tight editing; demonstrations on Meta must show value in under 15 seconds

**Activation quality signal**

VERY HIGH. DEMONSTRATION hooks attract owners who are already evaluating whether Tablr is right for them. They arrive with specific questions and a genuine interest in the product — not just the problem. This hook consistently produces the highest onboarding completion rate because the viewer has already previewed the workflow before signing up. Lower volume but higher downstream quality.

### 5. FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT

**What makes this hook work psychologically**

FOMO activates loss aversion — the psychological tendency to weight potential losses more heavily than equivalent gains. For restaurant owners, FOMO is acutely relevant: the idea that a competitor nearby is already using better marketing tools and pulling customers away is a credible and urgent threat. The hook doesn't need to prove the competitor is doing it — just make it believable. The frame is: "this is happening right now, with or without you."

**When to use**

- Funnel stage: awareness (cold audiences who haven't considered Tablr)
- High impact for scrappy_independent who is already sensitive to competitive pressure from larger brands
- New_owner who is afraid of losing the awareness race in their first year
- Works well when urgency is real and backed by a specific frame ("restaurant owners in your city")

**When to avoid**

- Do not use with community_focused persona — they actively resist the idea that they should care what other restaurants are doing
- Avoid overuse — FOMO hooks can generate clicks without generating genuine intent, leading to high CTR but weaker activation rates
- Do not make factual claims about what competitors are doing without being able to substantiate them
- Avoid dark, alarmist framing — Tablr's brand voice is empowering, not fear-based

**3 example hooks (Tablr restaurant context)**

1. "Restaurant owners in your neighborhood are already using AI to build loyalty programs, automate their marketing, and fill tables on slow nights. Are you?"
2. "While you were manually posting on Instagram, the restaurant two blocks away launched a loyalty campaign that brought back 140 lapsed customers last month."
3. "The independent restaurants that survive the next three years will own their customer relationship. The ones that don't will keep depending on apps that take 28% of every order."

**Example opening 3 seconds (video)**

Aerial shot of a busy restaurant street. Text appears over the scene: "47 restaurant owners in this city started their free Tablr trial this week." Cut to owner looking at screen. "Are you next?"

**Best personas**

- scrappy_independent (primary)
- new_owner (secondary — urgency of getting customers before competitors do)
- delivery_heavy (secondary — "your competitors are already off the apps" variant)

**Best channels**

- META (primary — cold audience discovery at scale)
- TIKTOK (secondary — urgency hooks work well in native short-form format)

**Expected CTR vs baseline**

- Meta: HIGH — FOMO is the highest-CTR hook type for scrappy_independent in synthetic data. Stop-scroll rate is strong.
- TikTok: HIGH — urgency and "are you doing this?" framing is native to TikTok content
- Note: high CTR does not guarantee high activation quality for this hook type (see below)

**Activation quality signal**

MEDIUM. FOMO hooks generate high click volume but attract a wider quality range. Some owners click from genuine urgency and activate quickly. Others click from anxiety but don't have enough clarity about what Tablr does to complete onboarding. Monitor onboarding_completion_rate for FOMO-hook traffic carefully. If CTR is high but onboarding drops below 40%, the hook is generating the wrong kind of attention. This is the primary hook type to watch for high-CTR, low-activation patterns in the synthetic data.

## Hook Selection Guide

A decision framework for picking the right hook based on persona and funnel stage.

```
START HERE: Who is the audience?
│
├── community_focused
│   ├── AVOID: CONTRAST, FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT
│   ├── Awareness stage? → SOCIAL_PROOF (peer owner story, authentic neighborhood restaurant)
│   └── Consideration stage? → DEMONSTRATION (show the tool respects their brand identity)
│
├── scrappy_independent
│   ├── Unaware / cold audience? → CONTRAST or FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT (stop the scroll)
│   ├── Problem-aware, hasn't tried tools? → SOCIAL_PROOF (reduce skepticism with peer proof)
│   └── Solution-aware, evaluating options? → DEMONSTRATION (prove simplicity)
│
├── new_owner
│   ├── Cold audience? → OUTCOME ("your first 100 loyal customers" framing)
│   ├── Engaged but hesitant? → SOCIAL_PROOF (another new owner who made it work)
│   └── Retargeting? → FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT (urgency to act before momentum is lost)
│
├── delivery_heavy
│   ├── Cold audience? → CONTRAST (delivery apps vs. direct ownership)
│   ├── Problem-aware? → OUTCOME (show what direct ordering and loyalty looks like)
│   └── Consideration stage? → FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT ("your competitors are moving off the apps")
│
└── growth_minded
    ├── Cold audience (LinkedIn)? → OUTCOME (ROI and consolidation framing)
    ├── Awareness stage (Meta)? → CONTRAST (efficiency gap vs. current tool stack)
    └── Consideration stage (YouTube)? → DEMONSTRATION (full product walkthrough)

SECONDARY DECISION: What funnel stage?
│
├── Unaware → CONTRAST or FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT (create urgency, name the problem)
├── Problem-aware → SOCIAL_PROOF or OUTCOME (validate the problem, show the destination)
├── Solution-aware → DEMONSTRATION or OUTCOME (differentiate, show the product at work)
└── Product-aware (retargeting) → SOCIAL_PROOF or DEMONSTRATION (remove final objections)

QUALITY CHECK: Will this hook attract buyers or window shoppers?
│
├── HIGH quality signal → DEMONSTRATION, OUTCOME (specific)
├── MEDIUM quality signal → SOCIAL_PROOF, CONTRAST
└── MIXED quality signal → FEAR_OF_MISSING_OUT (high CTR, monitor activation rate closely)
```

**One-line rule:** Match the hook to where the owner is in their thinking, not just where they are in your funnel. A scrappy independent who has never heard of Tablr needs a CONTRAST hook. The same owner who has watched your product demo twice needs SOCIAL_PROOF to close the gap.
