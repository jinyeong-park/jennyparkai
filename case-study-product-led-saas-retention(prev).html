The Problem

One Customer Journey, Five Disconnected Systems

A Fragmented Lifecycle: Signup and onboarding events live in the product analytics tool, trial state and plan changes live in billing, and churn reasons live in support notes — so no single query can answer "what actually happened to this customer, from signup to today."

Correlation Mistaken for Causation: Teams could see that certain early behaviors correlated with retention, but had no way to tell whether those behaviors caused retention, or simply selected for users who were already going to stick around.

Framework

The Connected Product Lifecycle

The model follows a single account and user from first touch through renewal, treating activation, trial conversion, paid retention, churn, and NRR as stages of one lifecycle rather than metrics owned by separate teams.

Signup→ Onboarding→ Activation→ Trial Conversion→ Paid Retention→ Churn / Expansion→ NRR

Approach

A Lifecycle Data Model & Experimentation Framework

1. Unified Lifecycle Model (SQL + dbt)

Modeled signup, onboarding events, trial state, subscription/billing status, and churn/support signals into one account-and-user-level lifecycle mart in dbt — with clearly defined activation, trial-conversion, and churn events so every downstream analysis uses the same definitions.

2. Activation Definition & Experimentation (Amplitude + Python)

  • Candidate Activation Milestones: Tested early actions — completing the core workflow, inviting a teammate, connecting an integration — for their relationship to 90-day retention.
  • A/B Tested Onboarding: Ran controlled experiments on onboarding flows (guided setup vs. self-serve) to validate which nudges causally move activation and trial conversion, rather than relying on cohort correlation alone.

3. Cohort & Survival Analysis (Python)

  • Kaplan-Meier Retention Curves: Modeled retention by cohort and activation tier to identify where and when accounts are most likely to churn.
  • NRR Waterfall: Decomposed net revenue retention into expansion, contraction, and churn by cohort to connect product behavior back to revenue.

Key Findings

Guided Onboarding Lifted Activation — But Paid Conversion Stayed Flat

The lifecycle funnel across 2,500 accounts identified two primary drop-off points: 35% of signups never created a workspace, and of those that did, fewer than half completed a qualifying key product action within 7 days. These two gaps — before and during onboarding — were the highest-leverage intervention points.

Lifecycle Stage Accounts Rate
Signups 2,500 100%
Workspace Created 1,615 65%
Activated (7-day window) 1,106 44%
Trial Started 738 30%
Paid Customers 766 31%
Retained 60-Day ~716 93.5% of eligible

The A/B experiment (onboarding_guided_v1) measured the guided flow against the existing onboarding across all 2,500 accounts. The activation lift was large and statistically significant — but the downstream check revealed a more nuanced picture.

Metric Control Variant Difference
Accounts 1,212 1,288 —
Activation rate 34.7% 53.3% +18.6 pp
Paid conversion rate 38.8% 38.7% −0.1 pp
60-day retention 94.3% 92.8% −1.5 pp

Segment-level analysis showed that SMB and Mid-Market accounts drove the bulk of the activation lift, while Enterprise showed a smaller, less certain response. The flat paid conversion result (−0.1 pp) was the key downstream check: activation lift that does not translate into monetization may reflect surface engagement rather than genuine product value.

From Insight to Action

Four Decisions the Analysis Enables

Activation Lift Quantified
Guided onboarding significantly improved activation for SMB and Mid-Market — with a constrained rollout recommendation for Enterprise until paid conversion is confirmed.
$96K
MRR Risk Surfaced
1,112 medium-risk accounts with active MRR identified through rules-based churn scoring — before any churn occurs, with CS actions mapped per segment.
Teammate Invite
Strongest Retention Signal
Accounts that invite teammates show the highest 60-day retention. Moving this prompt earlier in onboarding turns a downstream signal into an activation driver.
  • Product — Roll out guided onboarding to SMB and Mid-Market immediately. Keep Enterprise in a separate, implementation-led path — the activation lift is weaker and paid conversion remains unconfirmed at the enterprise level.
  • Product — Move teammate invite to before workspace setup completes. Collaboration adoption is the single strongest behavioral predictor of long-term retention in the data.
  • Analytics — Add paid conversion as a co-primary metric. Activation lift alone does not guarantee monetization. The experiment should not be declared a success until conversion holds.
  • Customer Success — Prioritize 1,112 medium-risk accounts. Run a scaled re-engagement sequence: use-case reminder, training offer, and product-adoption review. $96K in MRR is recoverable if intervened early.
